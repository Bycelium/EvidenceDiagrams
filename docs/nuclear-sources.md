# Nuclear-demo source audit

The demo inquiry asks whether nuclear electricity is dangerous in terms of human fatality risk across accidents, mining, worker exposure, and routine operation. It is an illustrative claim graph, not a systematic review. Conditional dBan impacts and factual-claim priors remain editable judgments.

This note records what the cited sources establish and the main scope limits kept in the preset. It does not turn reported cohort deaths, modeled counterfactual deaths, or projected cancers into observed attributable fatalities.

## Energy-system comparisons

- The [2007 Lancet review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0140673607612537) is the canonical source for the broad nuclear–coal–gas mortality comparison. It estimated 0.074 nuclear deaths per TWh in Europe.
- The [Our World in Data table](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/death-rates-from-energy-production-per-twh?tab=table) reports 24.62 deaths/TWh for coal, 2.82 for gas, 0.03 for nuclear, 0.04 for wind, and 0.02 for solar. Its [methodology](https://ourworldindata.org/safest-sources-of-energy) combines accidents and air pollution across sources, regions, and periods. Uncertainty in the low estimates prevents a reliable ranking of nuclear, wind, and solar.
- The same OWID fact feeds distinct fossil-fuel and renewable benchmarks. Reusing its claim and contribution IDs keeps their common input visible. The evaluator does not calculate a correlation discount between the two arguments.
- The [OECD/NEA report](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2018/06/the-full-costs-of-electricity-provision_g1g90cd4/9789264303119-en.pdf#page=118) reproduces the Lancet inputs in Table 5.2. It remains a reference path with zero additional conditional impact.
- The 2006 PSI paper [*Strengths and Weaknesses of Current Energy Chains in a Sustainable Development Perspective*](https://www.psi.ch/sites/default/files/import/ta/IlkEN/Hirschberg_etal_2006.pdf#page=7) reports normal-operation years of life lost per GWh for complete German energy chains. YOLL/GWh is not a death count, and severe accidents are tabulated separately.
- [Kharecha and Hansen (2013)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20140017100/downloads/20140017100.pdf) model 1.84 million net air-pollution deaths avoided worldwide during 1971–2009 by historical nuclear generation. Their [NASA summary](https://www.giss.nasa.gov/research/briefs/2013_kharecha_02/) explains the counterfactual. No individual death was observed as avoided.
- The [Henneman *Science* study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10870829/) models 460,000 U.S. coal-related excess deaths among adults aged 65 and older during 1999–2020. It supplies scoped evidence about coal beneath the broader fossil comparison and does not establish nuclear or gas rates.

Press articles remain relay metadata where relevant. They do not create another impact for the same argument.

## Chernobyl

- [UNSCEAR 2008 Annex D, corrected edition](https://www.unscear.org/unscear/uploads/documents/publications/UNSCEAR_2008_Annex-D-CORR.pdf) reports 134 acute-radiation-sickness cases and 28 deaths within three months. It also reports 6,848 thyroid cancers during 1991–2005 among people exposed under age 18 in Belarus, Ukraine, and four affected Russian regions, and 15 fatal thyroid-cancer cases by 2005. The thyroid count does not represent total accident mortality.
- The [UNSCEAR 2017 white paper](https://www.unscear.org/unscear/uploads/documents/publications/Chernobyl_WP_2017.pdf) reports 19,233 thyroid cancers during 1991–2015 for the same broad exposed-young population and estimates an attributable fraction with substantial uncertainty. It provides no updated death count.
- The [WHO Chernobyl Forum](https://www.who.int/news/item/05-09-2005-chernobyl-the-true-scale-of-the-accident) projected up to about 4,000 lifetime deaths among roughly 600,000 higher-exposed people. [IARC](https://www.iarc.who.int/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/IARCBriefingChernobyl.pdf) projected about 16,000 cancer deaths across Europe by 2065, with a 95% interval of 6,700–38,000. These are overlapping modeled projections with different scopes, not independent observed totals.
- The [HBO miniseries](https://www.hbo.com/chernobyl) is represented as a viewer-inference premise. Its [official companion podcast](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/the-chernobyl-podcast/id1459712981) documents production departures and a composite character.

## Fukushima

- The [IAEA accident report](https://www-pub.iaea.org/mtcd/publications/pdf/pub1710-reportbythedg-web.pdf) anchors the accident premise.
- [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/health-consequences-of-fukushima-nuclear-accident) reports no acute radiation injuries or deaths among workers or the public from accident radiation.
- [UNSCEAR's 2020/2021 findings](https://www.unscear.org/unscear/en/areas-of-work/fukushima-report-faq.html) report no documented adverse health effects among residents directly attributable to radiation exposure and state that a future increase is unlikely to be discernible. They also describe health harm associated with evacuation and social disruption. Absence of a discernible resident effect is not a claim of zero risk or zero disaster casualties.
- Japanese occupational compensation records include later recognized cancer cases. Compensation does not establish scientific cause and effect for an individual case, so the preset does not convert those awards into a separate observed-radiation-death count.

## Occupational exposure

- [PUMA](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10920468/) followed 119,709 male uranium miners over 4.3 million person-years and observed 7,754 lung-cancer deaths. That is the cohort total, not a radiation-attributable count.
- [INWORKS](https://www.bmj.com/content/382/bmj-2022-074520) followed 309,932 monitored workers over 10.7 million person-years and estimated a 52% increase in solid-cancer mortality per Gy with a ten-year lag (90% confidence interval 27%–77%). Its 28,089 observed solid-cancer deaths are also a cohort total.
- Historical dose, smoking, confounding, and transfer to modern public risk remain explicit limitations. The available descriptions do not establish that the two cohort populations are independent.

## Hydropower comparison

The [PSI severe-accident assessment](https://www.psi.ch/sites/default/files/import/ta/CeptEN/CETP_RA_Report.pdf#page=89) reports about 26,000 immediate floodwave deaths in the 1975 Banqiao and Shimantan failures. The count concerns a historical chain of dam failures and immediate deaths. It is not a general modern hydropower rate or a complete direct-plus-indirect disaster estimate.

## Additional researched route

The Shift Project's [world nuclear map dossier](https://theshiftproject.org/publications/cartographie-politique-nucleaire/) relays an IPCC conclusion on low health risks per unit of nuclear electricity. Its technical source is Hirschberg et al. 2016, DOI [10.1016/j.ress.2015.09.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ress.2015.09.013). An open [2014 PSAM conference precursor](https://www.iapsam.org/psam12/proceedings/paper/paper_546_1.pdf) documents the full-chain method. This route is not currently another preset relationship.
